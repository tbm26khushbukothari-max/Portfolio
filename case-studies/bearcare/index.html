Context

A D2C brand built for India's sneakerhead community.

Bearcare is a D2C brand serving India's growing sneakerhead culture with two product verticals: sneaker wellness (cleaning kits, sole protectors, restoration supplies) and sneaker crates (curated subscription boxes with accessories, laces, and care products). The founder had a clear product vision and a deep understanding of the community. What did not yet exist was a brand identity, a positioning framework, or a plan to reach the right people.

I led the brand identity and go-to-market strategy, from the brand architecture through to the 90-day launch playbook.

Problem

Sneaker care in India: a passionate audience with no brand that spoke their language.

India's sneakerhead community is small, vocal, and extremely particular. They can tell when a brand understands the culture and when it is faking it. Existing sneaker care products in the market were either generic cleaning supplies with no cultural connection, or expensive international imports. No Indian brand was speaking to the sneakerhead specifically.

The positioning challenge was to build something that felt native to sneaker culture, not borrowed from generic lifestyle branding. Authenticity was the only currency that mattered with this audience.

Approach

Build the brand from the culture up, not the product down.

The brand identity was anchored in sneaker culture's visual language: clean, precise, minimal, with a strong sense of craft. The naming (Bearcare) leaned into protection and care as a form of respect for the shoe, not just maintenance. Tone of voice was that of a fellow collector, not a cleaning product brand.

The GTM plan prioritised community seeding over paid acquisition in the first 30 days, reaching sneaker Discord servers, Instagram collector communities, and sneaker event attendees. Partnerships with Indian sneaker resellers and micro-influencers who already had the community's trust created earned awareness before the product was widely available.

The crate subscription was sequenced to launch at Day 60, after the wellness product line had established credibility. The crate gave repeat customers a reason to stay engaged and generated predictable revenue from the brand's most loyal buyers.

Impact

A launch-ready brand with two distinct but connected product verticals.

The 90-day roadmap delivered a complete brand system (identity, voice, visual guidelines), a community seeding strategy built around sneaker culture touchpoints, and a product launch sequence that prioritised credibility before scale. The crate subscription model created a second revenue stream with strong retention mechanics built in from day one.

Learnings

Build for the community first. The product follows.

Every decision in Bearcare's brand build was tested against one question: would a serious sneakerhead think this is real, or would they see through it? That filter eliminated a lot of generic brand thinking fast. The brand strategy at the 0 to 1 stage for a culture-driven product is not about finding the broadest possible audience. It is about being so specific to one community that they feel the brand was built for them alone.
